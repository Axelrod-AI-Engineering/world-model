Proposed implementation and ML stack for a concept-forming causal world model

Technical briefing · Axelrod World Model, v0 demo · October 2026 · Citations verified against publisher, Crossref, arXiv, or proceedings records; the few fields that could only be confirmed on a lab website are marked †.

Abstract. The demo shows a world model that (i) reads news and data, (ii) translates each item into graph-update operations whose referents start fuzzy and become concrete, (iii) grows taxonomies of objects and interactions, (iv) abduces recurring configurations of actors as nested schemas ("superentities"), (v) promotes reliable co-movements to causal links, (vi) maintains a daily belief state by learning from prediction error, and (vii) forecasts hypothetical interventions with calibrated uncertainty, explanations, and value-of-information rankings. The v0 code implements these functions with hand-specified signatures, templates, and Monte Carlo propagation. This briefing proposes how to implement each function at scale and on unseen language, grounding every choice in the cognitive science of concept formation, causal learning, analogy, and memory. Its core proposal is to make incremental, hierarchical, probabilistic concept formation, the Cobweb family as developed by the Teachable AI Lab, the substrate for every taxonomy, schema, and retrieval index in the system. Neural representation learning supplies perceptual inputs (text and series embeddings), and probabilistic state-space and causal-discovery methods carry the dynamics. Language models are confined to parsing and paraphrase under schema constraints; they never author causal claims or numbers.

1. Scope and design commitments

The functions to be implemented are those visible in the demo. Table 1 lists them with their current v0 mechanism. Three commitments shape every proposal below.

C1 · Concepts are formed incrementally, hierarchically, and probabilistically. Human category learning is incremental and order-sensitive, and it produces a privileged "basic" level of abstraction (Rosch, 1975; Rosch et al., 1976). Rational and network accounts treat categorization as prediction of unobserved features (Anderson, 1991; Love, Medin, & Gureckis, 2004). Every taxonomy in the system is therefore an incrementally formed concept hierarchy that supports prediction at any level, not a fixed ontology.

C2 · Concepts carry causal theories. Categories cohere because of the theories in which they participate (Murphy & Medin, 1985), and people categorize by reasoning over causal models of features (Rehder, 2003a, 2003b). The model's entities, interactions, and superentities are therefore causal structures with uncertain strengths and timing, and classification feeds causal priors.

C3 · Learning is driven by prediction error, with complementary fast and slow stores. Associative learning tracks surprise (Rescorla & Wagner, 1972) and is well described as Bayesian filtering over weights (Dayan & Kakade, 2001; Kruschke, 2008; Gershman, 2015). Rapid episodic storage complements slow semantic consolidation (Tulving, 1972; McClelland, McNaughton, & O'Reilly, 1995; Kumaran, Hassabis, & McClelland, 2016). This mirrors the demo's working memory, daily consolidation, and long-term memory.

2. Cognitive foundations

2.1 Concept formation as incremental probabilistic clustering

COBWEB (Fisher, 1987) forms a hierarchy of probabilistic concepts from a stream of attribute-value instances. Each node stores counts from which P(Ai = Vij | C) and P(C | parent) follow. Leaves are instances and internal nodes are prototype-like summaries. Placement is chosen to maximize category utility (CU), a measure Gluck and Corter (1985) proposed to explain the basic level:

CU({C₁…Cₙ}) = (1/n) Σₖ P(Cₖ) [ Σᵢ Σⱼ P(Aᵢ = Vᵢⱼ | Cₖ)² − Σᵢ Σⱼ P(Aᵢ = Vᵢⱼ)² ]

The squared terms are expected correct guesses under probability matching. The bracketed difference is a reduction in Gini impurity, and Corter and Gluck (1992) give the information-theoretic counterpart P(c)[H(F) − H(F | c)]. At each node an instance is incorporated by the best of four operators: add to the best child, create a new child, merge the two best children, or split the best child. Merge and split reduce, without removing, sensitivity to presentation order; Fisher (1996) adds iterative re-optimization. CLASSIT and COBWEB/3 extend the scheme to numeric attributes by substituting Gaussian densities, with an acuity floor on σ (Gennari, Langley, & Fisher, 1989; McKusick & Thompson, 1990). Iba and Langley (2011) review the family.

Two recent results matter for this system. First, Cobweb with the information-theoretic objective reproduces classic human category-learning effects. Predicting from leaves behaves exemplar-like and predicting from basic-level nodes behaves prototype-like (Lian, Varma, & MacLellan, 2024; cf. Medin & Schaffer, 1978; Nosofsky, 1986). The specificity at which the model answers is therefore a principled, tunable choice, not an artifact. Second, the Cobweb/4V and Cobweb/4L variants learn incrementally from images and text with little catastrophic forgetting, using the information-theoretic objective and multi-node prediction (Barari, Lian, & MacLellan, 2024, 2026; Lian, Baglodi, & MacLellan, 2024; Lian, Wang, & MacLellan, 2025). Barari, Kim, and MacLellan (2025) attribute this robustness to adaptive restructuring, sparse updates, and learning from information-theoretic sufficient statistics.

2.2 Concepts that carry causal theories

Causal-model theories of categorization hold that category membership is judged by how well an item's features fit a causal network (Rehder, 2003a, 2003b). On this view a superentity such as "credit tightening" is a schema whose roles are linked by causal relations with strengths and delays, not just a cluster of co-occurring entities. The world model's interactions encode the strength of each causal relation separately from the belief that it exists. This is the distinction between causal strength and causal structure that Griffiths and Tenenbaum (2005) show people make. It complements Cheng's (1997) power PC theory of strength estimation and the causal Bayes-net account of children's causal learning (Gopnik et al., 2004). Learning differs by direction: predictive and diagnostic learning show different cue competition (Waldmann & Holyoak, 1992). That is a reason to keep edge direction explicit and to treat the frontier's lag direction as evidence. Formal treatment of interventions and counterfactuals follows Pearl (2009) and Sloman (2005).

2.3 Relational structure, analogy, and schema induction

Analogy maps relational structure, preferring systems of connected relations over surface attributes (Gentner, 1983). The Structure-Mapping Engine computes such mappings (Falkenhainer, Forbus, & Gentner, 1989). MAC/FAC retrieves analogs in two stages, first a cheap content-vector filter and then structural evaluation (Forbus, Gentner, & Law, 1995). Comparing analogs induces abstract schemas that transfer to new problems (Gick & Holyoak, 1983), and the discovery of structural form can itself be framed as Bayesian model selection over graph grammars (Kemp & Tenenbaum, 2008; Tenenbaum, Kemp, Griffiths, & Goodman, 2011). Within the Cobweb family, LABYRINTH and TRESTLE bring components and relations into concept formation (Thompson & Langley, 1991; MacLellan, Harpstead, Aleven, & Koedinger, 2016):

  • Partial matching maps an instance's components onto a concept's.
  • Relations are flattened into attributes.
  • The result is categorized as in COBWEB/3.

Two newer pieces of work extend this further. One extends concept formation to chunks, typed entities in a specified relational configuration, implemented as the TRELLIS system (Singaravadivelan & Langley, 2026). The other gives structure mapping with tight bounds that scales to large nested domains (Weitekamp & MacLellan, 2026). Superentities, rivalries, supply chains, and composites of superentities are exactly such chunks.

2.4 Prediction error, filtering, and event segmentation

The Rescorla–Wagner rule updates associations in proportion to surprise. Kalman-filter accounts generalize it, carrying uncertainty about each weight and explaining phenomena such as backward blocking and "explaining away in weight space" (Dayan & Kakade, 2001; Kakade & Dayan, 2002; Kruschke, 2008; Gershman, 2015). The demo's fold step already treats an observation as a noisy measurement of a latent deviation and updates edge effects in proportion to their share of the prediction. The proposal makes this explicit as filtering in a state-space model (§4.7). Event segmentation theory places event boundaries at transient spikes in prediction error (Zacks, Speer, Swallow, Braver, & Reynolds, 2007). That gives a principled rule for cutting the stream of days into episodes for memory and recall, where v0 uses one episode per calendar day.

2.5 Episodic and semantic memory

Tulving's (1972) distinction maps onto prior states (dated episodes) and generalizations (taxonomies, schemas, entity memories). Complementary-learning-systems theory explains why both are needed: fast, pattern-separated storage avoids interference, while slow, interleaved consolidation extracts structure (McClelland et al., 1995; Kumaran et al., 2016). Cobweb offers a single substrate for both. Leaves are near-verbatim episodes, internal nodes are consolidated generalizations, and the same tree is the retrieval index (§4.5).

2.6 Simulation and inquiry

People predict physical outcomes by running approximate probabilistic simulations (Battaglia, Hamrick, & Tenenbaum, 2013). The forecaster is the same kind of engine: noisy, sampled propagation through a causal model, read out as distributions. Human inquiry is well modeled as choosing queries by expected informational value (Oaksford & Chater, 1994; Nelson, 2005), though people depart from it in documented ways (Coenen, Nelson, & Gureckis, 2019). This grounds the hypotheses board and its ranking. More broadly, compositional, causal, and learning-to-learn ingredients are what distinguish human-like learning from pattern recognition (Lake, Ullman, Tenenbaum, & Gershman, 2017).

3. Representations

3.1 Entities, subentities, and interactions as structured instances

Each graph object becomes a TRESTLE-style instance with four attribute types:

  • Nominal: kind, sector, region, category tags, attribute kind.
  • Numeric: a subentity's current z-score, volatility, observation frequency.
  • Component: an entity's subentities as named parts.
  • Relational: an interaction as a relation between components, for example (raises ?oil ?cpi) with numeric effect, delay, and lag.

Partial matching aligns an incoming instance's components with a concept's before categorization. Flattening then turns relations into attributes that category utility can score. Hidden attributes, such as an id or a free-text description, can be stored without entering the objective, as in the concept_formation library's underscore convention.

3.2 Language: embeddings organized by concept formation

Headlines, bodies, and entity descriptions are encoded with a sentence encoder (Reimers & Gurevych, 2019; cf. Devlin, Chang, Lee, & Toutanova, 2019; Mikolov, Chen, Corrado, & Dean, 2013). Because Cobweb/4V assumes conditionally independent dimensions within a concept, embeddings are whitened first. The Teachable AI Lab's cobweb-language-embedding package does exactly this:

  • PCAICAWhiteningModel applies PCA and then ICA, 256 dimensions by default.
  • The whitened vectors go into a continuous Cobweb tree, CPPCobwebTree. That class is a PyTorch translation of the C++ CobwebContinuousTree in the lab's core cobweb library, with label tables added.

On that tree, two applications are reported:

  • Hierarchical retrieval (Gupta, Singaravadivelan, & Wang, 2025). Matches dot-product search on strong encoders and stays robust on GPT-2 embeddings, where nearest-neighbor search degrades.
  • Lifelong hierarchical topic modeling (CobwebTM; Singaravadivelan, Gupta, Wang, & MacLellan, 2026). Needs no preset number of topics.

For this system, the same machinery organizes news into an incrementally growing tree of event types, so that "a kind of thing" in a news item is literally a node in a learned hierarchy. Cobweb/4L offers a complementary symbolic route. Its instances are an anchor word with proximity-weighted context, and it is competitive with CBOW on masked-word prediction in preliminary results (Lian, Baglodi, & MacLellan, 2024; MacLellan, Matsakis, & Langley, 2022). That route suits learning the model's own vocabulary of entity aliases incrementally.

3.3 Hybrid instances and hierarchy-aware embeddings

A single Cobweb node can hold count tables for nominal attributes alongside mean and variance tensors for embedding attributes, as Cobweb/4V does for labels and images. This lets one tree categorize an entity jointly by its symbolic signature and by the embedding of its description. Where geometric embeddings of hierarchies are wanted, for instance for neural edge proposal, three families encode partial order directly:

  • Hyperbolic (Poincaré) embeddings (Nickel & Kiela, 2017).
  • Order embeddings (Vendrov, Kiros, Fidler, & Urtasun, 2016).
  • Box-lattice embeddings (Vilnis, Li, Murty, & McCallum, 2018).

The lab's neuro-symbolic work bridges the two worlds:

  • Taxonomic networks are one representation shared by a symbolic and a neural learner (Wang, Haarer, Barari, & MacLellan, 2025).
  • Deep taxonomic networks discover prototype hierarchies with a tree-structured mixture prior (Wang, Haarer, Zhu, Dai, & MacLellan, 2025).
  • The modes of a diffusion model's noisy marginals form a hierarchy corresponding to a Cobweb tree, with the basic level at an intermediate noise level (Wang, Singaravadivelan, & MacLellan, 2026).

These are the natural routes for learning the representation and the hierarchy jointly once data volume justifies it (Bengio, Courville, & Vincent, 2013; Higgins et al., 2017; van den Oord, Li, & Vinyals, 2018).

3.4 The causal graph itself

Graph neural networks (Kipf & Welling, 2017; Battaglia et al., 2018) can provide priors for link proposal: which entity pairs are plausibly connected, given their types and neighborhoods. Causal representation learning (Schölkopf et al., 2021) frames the longer-term goal of learning the latent variables, the subentities, from raw series rather than specifying them. In this proposal both remain subordinate to explicit, inspectable causal structure: neural models propose; review and evidence decide.

4. Proposed implementation, function by function

4.1 F1 · Translating news into graph-update operations

Retrieve, then parse under constraint. The incoming item is embedded and categorized in the event-type tree (§3.2). Best-first multi-node categorization returns a candidate set of event concepts, entities, and interaction types, each with a score. An instruction-tuned language model then fills a JSON schema identical to the GraphUpdateEvent operation grammar. Generation is restricted by constrained decoding, and every end must be either a retrieved candidate or an explicit "kind of thing" node; the model must also return the text spans that justify each operation. Few-shot examples come from reviewed translations: each approval or edit in the Review panel is a labeled pair, retrieved by the same Cobweb index, so the system improves with use. The language model never sets magnitudes for causal effects. It extracts stated quantities, such as "50 bp" or "+35%", and the model's priors supply everything else.

4.2 F2 · Fuzzy-to-real refinement

Each op end is an instance: the span's embedding, its predicted attribute kind, and context such as the other end and the relation. It is sorted down the object or interaction tree, and the root-to-leaf path is the fuzzy-to-real trajectory the walkthrough visualizes. The refiner stops at the deepest node whose posterior exceeds a confidence threshold. Following Cobweb/4V's prediction, nodes are ranked by collocation, which combines cue validity and category validity, and the expanded nodes' predictions are mixed (Barari et al., 2024):

s(c) = P(c | x) · P(x | c)

Two rules follow:

  • Default specificity: the basic level, the node on the path with the highest category utility (Corter & Gluck, 1992). A leaf is used only when evidence is strong.
  • Pending refinements: an end that cannot reach a concrete subentity waits at its node with the candidate distribution, as in v0. Later evidence re-sorts it.

4.3 F3 · Growing the taxonomies

Object and interaction taxonomies become Cobweb trees updated by the four operators under the information-theoretic objective:

CU = (1/n) Σₖ P(Cₖ) [ H(A | parent) − H(A | Cₖ) ], with H = ½ Σ_d log(2πe σ²_d) for Gaussian attributes

This replaces v0's hand-written split rule with a learned one. A new node appears exactly when partitioning increases predictive information. Inherited priors stay: an entity classified under "central bank" starts with the central-bank node's effect and timing distributions, a form of hierarchical shrinkage. Periodic re-optimization keeps the tree from drifting with presentation order (Fisher, 1996); in the lab's code these are redistribute, insert_node, and remove_node. Every structural change still passes through the demo's single writer and its review policy.

4.4 F4 · Abducing superentities as chunks

An active day's subgraph becomes a structured instance:

  • Components: the entities that moved.
  • Relations: the edges that carried the move.
  • Attributes: magnitudes and signs.

TRESTLE-style partial matching against existing schema concepts, accelerated by bounded structure mapping (Weitekamp & MacLellan, 2026), either increments a schema's support or, through CU, forms a new one. Gick and Holyoak's (1983) finding that comparison induces schemas is operationalized directly: a candidate superentity is admitted after repeated, spaced matches, as in v0's support counting. Admission then goes to human review. Nested superentities, such as stagflation built from credit tightening and an energy shock, are chunks whose components are themselves chunks (Singaravadivelan & Langley, 2026). Shared members, an entity owned by two superentities, fall out naturally because partial matching allows one entity to fill roles in several schemas. The demo now visualizes this sharing explicitly.

4.5 F5 · Recalling analogs

Recall follows MAC/FAC (Forbus et al., 1995):

  1. MAC (fast filter). Best-first categorization of the current episode in the episode tree. This is a hierarchical, prototype-based retriever of the kind evaluated by Gupta et al. (2025).
  2. FAC (structural evaluation). Align the shortlisted episodes and schemas with the current situation, and rank them by systematicity. Each recalled schema and analog carries its alignment, so the explanation can state why a 2008-like or 2023-like bank run was recalled.

4.6 F6 · From co-movement to causal link

The correlation frontier is retained as a hypothesis queue, but its statistic changes. Lagged associations are tested with PCMCI (Runge, Nowack, Kretschmer, Flaxman, & Sejdinovic, 2019), which conditions on each series' own past and on common drivers. This controls the autocorrelation and confounding that make raw lagged correlations unreliable (cf. Granger, 1969). Promotion uses Bayesian causal support, the evidence for a link's existence rather than its strength (Griffiths & Tenenbaum, 2005), which is computed together with strength estimates. A promoted link enters with low existence probability, as in v0, and it accrues or loses belief through the same filtering as every other edge. Human approval remains mandatory, because observational time series cannot settle direction or confounding on their own (Pearl, 2009).

4.7 F7 · The daily belief state

Subentity deviations are latent states in a linear-Gaussian state-space model, or a particle filter where effects are threshold-moderated. Observations from data releases are noisy measurements with the series' own noise. Edge effects follow a Bayesian distributed-lag model whose kernels are gamma-shaped delays, as in v0. Their posteriors are updated by the filter's prediction error, which recovers Kalman-filter associative learning as a special case (Dayan & Kakade, 2001; Gershman, 2015). Daily propagation is the filter's predict step, which is why unobserved values, such as NVIDIA's never-reported capex in the demo, still move. Episode boundaries are placed at prediction-error spikes rather than at midnight (Zacks et al., 2007), and each episode is stored as a Cobweb instance for recall.

4.8 F8 · Forecasting at several granularities

The v0 skeleton is retained, because it matches the simulation account of prediction (Battaglia et al., 2013). The steps are:

  1. Isolate the networks the question touches.
  2. Predict each by Monte Carlo along kernels.
  3. Couple them.
  4. Mix over rival structures with weights from track record, which is Bayesian model averaging over schemas.

The proposal changes two inputs. Which schemas to isolate becomes a Cobweb recall, so unfamiliar questions still find the nearest structures. Scenario weights become posterior model probabilities updated by scored outcomes. Coarse targets, an actor, a sector, a superentity, or a category, are read out as distributions of members' average standardized effects, sample by sample, as the demo already does. Calibration is monitored with CRPS and interval coverage and, where coverage drifts, adjusted by split-conformal recalibration on past forecasts.

4.9 F9 · Explanations and forecast briefs

Path attribution, the share of each causal path in an effect, is complemented by counterfactual ablation: rerun the forecast with an edge or schema removed, using common random numbers (Pearl, 2009). The brief's facts are computed as they are in the demo:

  • the bottom line;
  • a timeline of onsets and peaks;
  • who is affected;
  • the news that shaped the forecast and the news since, scored by overlap with its drivers;
  • tracking against the forecast's bands;
  • what to watch.

A language model may only paraphrase these into prose under a checker that every number and entity in the text appears in the computed facts.

4.10 F10 · Ranking hypotheses by value of information

The v0 score multiplies forecast sensitivity by an uncertainty term:

VOI(h) ≈ sensitivity(h) × 4p(1 − p)

The proposal generalizes it to expected information gain about the watched forecasts, the Bayesian measure that best fits human question-asking in many tasks (Nelson, 2005; Oaksford & Chater, 1994). The board would then suggest which data release or analyst query would most reduce forecast uncertainty. Known departures of human inquiry from this ideal (Coenen et al., 2019) are useful when presenting suggestions to people.

4.11 F11 · Asking about a brief

The chat window answers by retrieval over the brief, the trace events of the question's scope, and the model state. Answers must cite trace events or brief facts and cannot modify the model; questions that require new computation are converted into new theses, which go through F8. Until that is built, the demo's window is a labeled mock-up.

6. Evaluation plan

Cognitive fidelity. Does the system show the regularities that motivate the design?

  • Basic-level preference in refinement.
  • Typicality gradients in classification.
  • Exemplar-to-prototype shifts with experience (Lian et al., 2024).
  • Schema transfer after comparison (Gick & Holyoak, 1983).
  • Graceful learning with little forgetting under distribution shift (Barari et al., 2026).

Predictive quality. CRPS, interval coverage, and omission and commission against realized outcomes, reported separately for single values and for actors, sectors, superentities, and categories.

Translation. Accuracy of operations and ends against reviewed gold translations, and the share of ends that reach a concrete subentity at each specificity level.

Human factors. Review load, approval and edit rates, time-to-decision, and whether briefs and walkthroughs improve users' predictions of the model's behavior.

7. Risks and open questions

  • Order effects. Incremental hierarchies depend on presentation order. Merge, split, and periodic re-optimization mitigate this, but replay-based tests are needed (Fisher, 1996).
  • Distributional assumptions. Diagonal-Gaussian concepts misfit correlated embeddings. Whitening is necessary, and full or low-rank covariances are worth evaluating.
  • Causal identifiability. Observational series rarely identify direction or exclude confounding. PCMCI reduces, but does not remove, false links; human review and intervention-like natural experiments remain essential.
  • Language-model errors. Constrained decoding prevents malformed operations, not wrong ones. Review and span grounding are the safeguards.
  • Scale. Cobweb's cost per instance grows with branching factor and depth. Insert-only modes and branch and depth limits, both available in the lab's code, bound it.
  • Data. Everything in the demo is synthetic. Real deployment requires licensed data and an audit of what each source can support.
